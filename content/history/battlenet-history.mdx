Diablo II's online play ran through Battle.net, Blizzard's free online service. How it worked shaped the game's culture, its economy and its long history of cheating.

## Open and closed realms

Diablo II offered two kinds of online play.

- **Open Battle.net** stored characters on your own computer. You could take them online and play with others, but nothing stopped a player editing their save file. Open games were known for edited items and characters.
- **Closed realms** stored characters on Blizzard's servers. The server ran the game, so characters and items couldn't be edited locally. This made trading meaningful and gave the realms an economy.

## Ladder

Ladder seasons reset characters periodically. Everyone started again at the same time, raced to the top of the rankings, and had access to ladder-only content. When a season ended, ladder characters moved to the non-ladder realm. Private servers later built their whole format around seasons.

## The economy

Closed realms produced a real economy. Players traded items for other items, and particular items became currency, from Stones of Jordan to high runes. Duplicated items, bugs and botting repeatedly distorted that economy. Some players traded items for real money, and whole sites grew up around trading.

## Later years

After Diablo II Resurrected launched in 2021, most of the official player base moved to it. Blizzard's legacy realms remain online, but most legacy multiplayer happens on [private servers](/guides/private-servers-explained).
