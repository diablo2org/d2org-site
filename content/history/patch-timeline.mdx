Diablo II was patched for sixteen years. Some patches were small fixes. A few changed the game completely. This timeline covers the ones that matter today. Each version has its own page with more detail.

<VersionSummary />

## 2000 to 2001: launch and expansion

Diablo II launched in June 2000 with five classes and four acts. Early patches came quickly as Blizzard fixed bugs and exploits and dealt with the realm economy.

Lord of Destruction arrived with 1.07 in June 2001. It added the Assassin and Druid, Act V, runewords and 800×600 resolution. Patch 1.08 followed, and 1.09 that same year brought a major rebalance. Version 1.09 then stayed current for about two years, which is why so many older tools and mods target it.

## 2003: 1.10 and synergies

Patch 1.10 was the largest change after the expansion. Skill synergies meant skills now boosted each other, which reshaped almost every build. It also added Uber Diablo and the Annihilus charm, plus a set of ladder-only runewords including Enigma. Most of the game's modern balance dates from here.

## 2005 to 2010: endgame and quality of life

Patch 1.11 added Uber Tristram and the Hellfire Torch. Patch 1.12 removed the need for the CD. Patch 1.13 added respecs through Akara and the Token of Absolution, and 1.13c became the version the community settled on for mods and private servers.

## 2016: 1.14 and modern Windows

After years without updates, Blizzard released 1.14 to keep the game running on modern Windows. It merged the game's DLLs into a single `Game.exe`. That fixed compatibility for ordinary players and broke much of the modding ecosystem, which stayed on 1.13c. Patch 1.14d is the last legacy patch.
